## 0. TL;DR (pass 2; read this first)

**Where we stand (PROJECT_STATE.md, 2026-09-25):**
- The best fold-0 exact macro-F0.5 is **0.98703**. With the given candidates the blocking oracle is **0.99645**, so the whole matching-plus-decision gap is about **0.0094**.
- The residual errors are of two kinds:
  - (a) **FN on empty-address targets with shared names**, which are often undecidable;
  - (b) **FP on planted sibling look-alikes**: same name, nearby house number, same street. The generator *also* shifts the house numbers of true copies.
- **Modal compute is blocked** by the spend limit, and GPU needs billing. Any LLM stage is therefore gated on compute. The CPU-only items below come first.

1. **What the literature supports: a small fine-tuned model is about equal to an encoder in-domain; its gains come under shift.**
   - [P1] Steiner & Bizer 2026, trained on the benchmark sets: Qwen3-8B beats Ditto by +3.71 / +4.71 F1 on product data and by about 0 on bibliographic data. Qwen3-0.6B is −14.80 on WDC.
   - [P2] **Zhang et al., EDBT 2025, cross-dataset (leave-one-dataset-out), mean F1 over 11 datasets:**
     - AnyMatch[Llama-3.2-1B] 87.5 vs **Ditto 72.9** vs GPT-4 87.4.
     - **On the two restaurant datasets (the closest public analogue to business listings): 99.6 vs 92.5 (Fodors-Zagats) and 98.2 vs 78.5 (Zomato-Yelp).**
   - The US/India slice is in-domain with millions of labels, so expect a small gain there. **France (15 % of test S1, unseen) is where a small generative matcher earns its cost.**
2. **Our residual errors are the ones the literature says language models do *not* fix.**
   - No LLM-EM paper I found tests "same name, same street, nearby house number" negatives where true copies are also perturbed.
   - HELEA 2026 [P2] shows that name-dependent matchers "collapse to near-random" on same-name hard negatives. The fix there was *structure* (relational context), not a bigger LM.
   - For us that structure is already in the collective sibling features and the planned house-number geometry features (features v2). **Do features v2 before any LLM work.**
   - Undecidable empty-address targets stay undecidable for any model.
3. **"LLM only on the ambiguous band" is well supported as a pattern, but its size is baseline-dependent.**
   - [P2] Lin 2026: on 2,518 pairs where a tuned fuzzy matcher was uncertain, logistic regression over 4 similarity scores got **0.596 F1**, while hosted LLMs got **0.877-0.961**. The baseline is weak (4 features, which the author says), so this is an upper-end estimate.
   - [P2] Ather 2026 (Statistics Canada): open-source LLMs only on the most uncertain pairs, **combined with the prior match probability by a Bayesian update**.
   - For us this means: **fuse the LLM score into the GBDT in log-odds space, fitted on OOF band pairs.** Do not replace the GBDT probability.
4. **The per-target "select one S1 or NONE" head works, but it must be fine-tuned.**
   - [P2] AvengER (ESWC 2025) uses SELECT prompts with an explicit `[0]` = none. **Mistral-7B-v0.3, which is Apache-2.0 and compliant, fine-tuned: mean F1 0.88, against 0.67 pre-trained. Llama-3.1-8B goes 0.72 → 0.88.**
   - Fine-tuning roughly **halves position-bias variance** (0.0061 vs 0.0116). It keeps 0.88 even with **all k=10 blocking candidates** in the prompt.
   - Random few-shot examples *hurt* the pre-trained model (0.73 → 0.63).
   - Fine-tuned models barely complement each other: a majority-vote ensemble scores 0.88, the same as one model. **So train one model and do not build an ensemble.**
5. **Tune and validate only at full scale.**
   - [P2] Shrestha 2026: a few-shot prompt revision that looked better on a small validation sample *dropped* full-set F1 from 0.948 to 0.914.
   - When lexical signals are strong, rules and a zero-shot LLM are equal (0.950 vs 0.948 on Abt-Buy).
   - Majority voting over 5 runs added 0.005 at 5× the cost.
6. **Carried over from pass 1, and still valid:**
   - CE-RAG4EM: several decisions in one prompt make the model match-biased, which is bad for F0.5.
   - Beyond Scale: larger heads learn name-agreement shortcuts (cross-encoder precision fell from 96.9 to 76.4).
   - OpenSanctions Pairs: the feature matcher over-matches (14.1 % FP), while LLMs fail on cross-script. So feed the romanized fields.
   - Serving cost varies 28× across stacks, so benchmark our own stack on 10K pairs first.
7. **Training-set composition for any neural band model:**
   - Pairs the GBDT is uncertain about (AnyMatch, Steiner, active learning).
   - **Plus confident errors**: ALER 2026's "confused + confident" query strategy.
   - **Plus about 20 % random pairs** to undo selection bias: Steiner & Bizer ICDE-W 2026 do exactly this. There, LLM-labelled active-learning training data matched human labels on company records (0.939 vs 0.954 and 0.897 vs 0.898 F1).

## 1. Where an LLM stage fits in *our* pipeline (constraints recap, updated with project state)

- **Candidate pool.**
  - Test features exist for **88.4M pairs (51 per S1)**. Train has 38.0M pairs over folds 0-3.
  - Blocking recall is 0.9905 and the oracle is 0.9972 (budget-pruned).
  - The ambiguous band of "0.3 < p < 0.7, about 1-3 % of pairs" (models.md) is **about 0.9 to 2.7M test pairs**. That is my arithmetic on those two figures. It must be measured.
- **Metric.** Per-S1 F0.5, macro. Precision errors (a sibling or distractor accepted) hurt more than misses. Singletons (5.6 %) score 1 only for an empty prediction, so **NONE decisions are first-class**.
- **Decision layer facts.**
  - Probabilities are well calibrated at train density.
  - Test is denser, so the best threshold drifts from 0.7 to 0.8 and mid-range probabilities become over-confident.
  - Expected-F and π-hurdle rules did **not** beat a tuned threshold.
  - So any LLM score must be (i) fused in log-odds space and (ii) re-thresholded on **P-dense**, not on P-main.
- **Residual error classes and what an LM can plausibly add:**

  | Error class | Can an LM help? | Evidence | Better first move |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Sibling look-alike FP (same name, same street, nearby number) | Little in pairwise mode. A listwise head sees the sibling, but true copies also have shifted numbers, so it is ambiguous by construction | HELEA: name-reliant models collapse on same-name negatives. Beyond Scale: scale amplifies name shortcuts | House-number geometry features (features v2); collective and sibling features; P2-7 augmentation below |
  | Empty-address, shared-name FN | Only where name-level cues exist (alias/DBA, legal suffix, acronym). Otherwise undecidable | OpenSanctions: LLMs are strong on name variants but fail on cross-script | Neighbour imputation from sibling copies (contests.md #4) |
  | France (unseen country) | **Yes, the most plausible gain** | Deep Dive cross-dataset +14.6 mean F1 over Ditto. Beyond Scale: the generative head retains 83.6 % across datasets | BEACON-style reweighting (CPU) plus a band LM when there is GPU |
  | Cross-script Indic | Only if the romanized fields are fed alongside the raw ones | OpenSanctions error analysis | Keep the translit/dictionary layer as the primary fix |
- **Hard rules.** Final models must be MIT/Apache-2.0 and ≤ 8B, with no external lookup or augmentation.
  - This rules out Wikidata/KB RAG (CE-RAG4EM), hosted teachers (GPT-x, Claude, Gemini, Kimi), LLM-generated external knowledge (the LLM-HN/GPT-4o-mini negatives, the GPT-5.2 value mappings) and Jellyfish (CC-BY-NC).
  - **Open question for the rules:** is a *training-time-only* teacher a "final model"? The conservative reading is that every model used anywhere must be ≤ 8B and Apache/MIT.
- **Compute.** Modal is blocked, and the laptop must not run models. Every GPU item below is conditional on the user adding billing or approving a Kaggle/Colab route.

### 2.1 Steiner & Bizer, *Labeling Training Data for Entity Matching Using Large Language Models* (arXiv 2606.28823, Jun 2026)

- **What.** Uni Mannheim (the MatchGPT group). The LLM is a teacher that labels selected candidate pairs, and cheaper students are trained on
  those labels. Students: Ditto/RoBERTa, **XGBoost over similarity features**, and **Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/8B with LoRA**.
  Code: https://github.com/wbsg-uni-mannheim/Automatic-data-labeling.
- **Primary.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28823 (PDF text extracted and tables read).
- **Technique.**
  - Pair selection by (a) similarity search, (b) active learning with a feature-based committee, (c) active learning with a Ditto
    committee.
  - Teachers: GPT-5.2, Qwen 3.6 Plus, Kimi K2.6.
  - Post-processing: an evidence-based "relabeling" self-review, and closure-based dropping of *bridge edges* (match pairs whose removal
    splits a connected component).
- **Evidence (Table 4, trained on the benchmark set, F1).**

  | Benchmark | XGBoost | Ditto | Qwen3-0.6B | Qwen3-1.7B | Qwen3-8B |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Abt-Buy | 45.54 | 88.34 | 86.17 | 88.32 | 92.05 |
  | Walmart-Amazon | 61.13 | 85.66 | 84.21 | 85.84 | 90.37 |
  | WDC Products | 36.61 | 71.94 | 57.14 | 67.05 | 73.53 |
  | DBLP-ACM | 92.76 | 98.43 | 98.17 | 98.61 | 98.46 |
  | DBLP-Scholar | 55.01 | 95.64 | 94.72 | 95.10 | 94.94 |

  - Zero-shot Qwen3: 0.6B gets 35.74 (Abt-Buy), 24.45 (WA) and 40.95 (WDC); 8B gets 85.31 / 73.33 / 66.14.
  - **Fine-tuning is essential for small models.**
  - Mean trained F1: XGBoost 63.34, Ditto 87.52, Qwen3 students 83.67 to 88.05.
  - XGBoost trails Ditto by 19 to 35 F1 on four of five benchmarks. **Caveat:** its features are only per-attribute token Jaccard, numeric
    closeness and the cosine of text-embedding-3-small embeddings (100 trees, depth 6). That is far weaker than our ~90 engineered
    features, so do **not** read it as "GBDT loses".
  - Machine labels are fit for use. The best machine-labeled set against the benchmark set differs by +0.11 (Ditto), +0.60 (0.6B),
    −1.08 (1.7B) and −1.61 (8B) F1.
  - Active learning (Ditto) raises the positive rate by up to +12.74 pp and selects more hard positives.
  - Teacher error rates on a 1,028-pair human audit (1.60 to 4.49 %) are close to the gold-label error rates (0.53 to 2.52 %).
  - Relabeling helps product data most (Abt-Buy +1.56). Closure-based variants "do not consistently help".
  - **Runtime (Table 7, per 1M pairs):**

    | Model | Time per 1M pairs | vs Ditto |
    |---|---|---|
    | XGBoost | 5.5 min | |
    | Ditto (A40, batch 64) | 37.3 min | 1× |
    | Qwen3-0.6B LoRA | 24.3 h | 39.1× |
    | Qwen3-1.7B LoRA | 27.7 h | 44.6× |
    | Qwen3-8B LoRA | 36.7 h | 59.1× |

    The paper says training used a single 48 GB GPU. The Qwen3 serving stack is not stated.
- **Adaptation.**
  - We have gold train labels, so the "teacher" part matters only transductively (France / cross-script test pairs; see 2.6 and §4 P2).
  - What transfers directly:
    1. **Active-learning-style pair selection** for the neural stage. Use pairs where our GBDT committee (LightGBM seeds or folds)
       disagrees or is uncertain.
    2. The expected in-domain gain of a small generative model over a well-trained encoder is **0 to 5 F1 on hard data and about 0 on
       clean data**.
    3. **Bridge-edge dropping did not help consistently.** Keep our graph cleanup gated by validation.
- **Priority: P1** (selection recipe, and the realistic gain and cost anchors).

### 2.2 Smith, Sesodia, Lindenberg, Schroeder de Witt, *OpenSanctions Pairs: Large-Scale Entity Matching with LLMs* (arXiv 2603.11051, v1 Feb 2026, v2 Aug 2026)

- **What.** A real-world compliance benchmark with cross-script names. It compares LLMs against OpenSanctions' production matcher
  "RegressionV1": 18 features covering name similarity (token overlap, Levenshtein, phonetic), dates, identifiers and demographics.
- **Primary.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11051 (abstract v2) and https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11051v1 (tables).
- **Evidence.**
  - The v2 abstract says "755,540 expert-labeled pairs over 1 million entities, aggregated from 293 source datasets across 45
    jurisdictions". The v1 HTML says 31 countries. It also gives entity counts: Person 320K, Company 48K, Organization 47K, with 76.9 %
    positives.
  - F1: **RegressionV1 91.33**; Llama-3.1-8B 0-shot 94.05, optimized 95.94; DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B 97.76, optimized 98.23
    (P 98.60 / R 97.86); GPT-4o 98.95.
  - Optimization used DSPy MIPROv2 with 200 pairs and 15 candidate programs.
  - **Error analysis:** the rule/feature system over-matches (14.1 % FP), DeepSeek has 3.0 % FP, and "LLMs primarily fail on cross-script
    transliteration and minor identifier/date inconsistencies".
  - Latency: about 70 s vs about 25 s per example (14B vs 8B), as stated in v1. The unit looks odd, so do not reuse it for planning.
- **Adaptation.**
  - The closest public analogue to our task: business and person names, multi-script, a feature-based incumbent.
  - It predicts that an LLM on the band mostly **removes false positives** from a feature matcher, which is what F0.5 rewards. It will
    *not* fix cross-script pairs unless we feed it our transliterated fields alongside the raw ones. **Serialize both raw and
    romanized name and address in the prompt.**
  - **Neither model is compliant** (Llama license; the 14B is over the cap). Use Qwen3-4B or Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
- **Priority: P1** (the evidence that justifies a band-only precision-fixer).

### 2.3 Guo, Klein, Huang, *CaRL-EM: Cost-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Entity Matching with LLMs* (arXiv 2609.01195, Sep 2026; the arXiv page states it was accepted to ACL 2026)

- **What.** A PPO policy (3-layer MLP) chooses among the operators MATCH (pairwise, Flan-T5-xl), COMPARE (pairwise preference),
  SELECT (listwise over k=4 candidates, "or None") and DECIDE. The reward is quality minus λ·cost, with potential-based shaping.
- **Primary.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01195 (PDF text extracted).
- **Evidence.**
  - Table 2: CaRL-EM was trained once on Abt-Buy, then zero-shot on 7 datasets. **F1macro 76.09 at $0.13**, against:
    - COMEM 74.25 at $0.59;
    - "Selecting" 69.94;
    - "Matching" 46.66;
    - **Ditto in-domain (trained per dataset) 85.64 at $2.22**.
  - Table 3: Ditto trained on AB and transferred scores **59.75**.
  - Table 3, with CaRL-EM's backend swapped to **Llama-3.1-8B: F1macro 69.36 (F1id 81.23, F1none 57.49)**. With GPT-oss-20B: 79.14.
  - Metric definition: F1id covers anchors that have a true match and F1none covers anchors that have none. Selecting a wrong
    candidate counts as FP+FN, which "aligns mathematically with the standard pairwise F1" under single-match validity.
- **Adaptation.**
  1. Adopt **their F1id / F1none split as a validation diagnostic** for our per-target decisions. F1none is our distractor/NONE quality
     (26 % of targets).
  2. The *structure* worth copying is: **global SELECT first, then a local pairwise check before accepting, and NONE when evidence is
     weak.** In our terms: listwise head picks the best S1 → pairwise verifier p(match) → expected-F0.5 accept.
  3. The RL controller itself is overkill for us; our GBDT margin already routes.
- **Priority: P2** for the select-then-verify structure and the F1none diagnostic. The RL controller is P3.

### 2.4 Ma, Zhang, Khan, Schelter, Groth, *Cost-Efficient RAG for Entity Matching with LLMs: A Blocking-based Exploration* (CE-RAG4EM, arXiv 2602.05708, Feb 2026)

- **What.** Retrieval-augmented EM. Retrieval pulls **Wikidata** entities, predicates and triples (Jina v3 embeddings, BFS/expansion). It
  uses blocking-based *batch retrieval* (one retrieval per q-gram block, max block size 6) and *batch generation* (several pairs per
  prompt). Backbones: GPT-4o-mini, Gemini-2.0-Flash-Lite, and **Qwen3-4B / Qwen3-8B under vLLM**.
- **Primary.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05708 (PDF text extracted).
- **Evidence.**
  - Table 3 against plain LLM-EM. F1 rose, for example DBGO 80.77 vs 65.02 and AMGO 55.47 vs 48.65. **On AMGO, precision fell by
    17.60 points while recall rose by 55.00.**
  - Against Ditto/Unicorn (leave-one-out) it is better on WAAM, ITAM and WDC and worse on DBAC, BEER and AMGO.
  - **Key finding on batching:** batch generation "tends to increase recall but can reduce precision". It "introduce[s] cross-pair
    coupling… the model adopts a more 'match-biased' decision rule for consistency within the batch". Batch retrieval with per-pair
    generation preserves precision.
- **Adaptation.**
  - Wikidata RAG is **not allowed** (external data).
  - The transferable lesson is negative and important for F0.5: **never put several independent decisions in one generation.** Reuse
    context through vLLM prefix caching (the S1 record as a shared prefix) and emit one decision per target.
- **Priority: P2** (a design constraint, zero cost).

### 2.5 Wadhwa, Krishnan, Wang, Wallace, Kong, *Learning from Natural Language Explanations for Generalizable Entity Matching* (EMNLP 2024 main, pp. 6114-6129; Amazon authors)

- **What.** EM is recast as conditional generation. A small **Flan-T5-base** (Apache-2.0) is trained to emit the label plus a
  CoT-style explanation, where the explanations were elicited from Mistral-7B-Instruct / Alpaca / GPT-4 via 2-shot ICL.
- **Primary.** https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.352/ and https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09330 (PDF text extracted).
- **Evidence.**
  - **In-domain (Table 1), Flan-T5-base vs Ditto:**

    | Dataset | Flan-T5-base | Ditto |
    |---|---|---|
    | Abt-Buy | 89.92 | 89.33 |
    | Amazon-Google | 76.23 | 75.58 |
    | Walmart-Amazon | 87.40 | 86.76 |
    | Beer | 93.33 | 94.37 |
    | iTunes-Amazon | 93.09 | 97.06 |
    | WDC-Computers | 92.08 | 91.70 |

    So a generative small model is about the same as the encoder in-domain.
  - **Out of domain:** explanation-augmented training raised F1 by an average of 22.32 (cross-schema), 14.47 (cross-domain) and 13.67
    (cross-distribution). For example, WDC-All→Abt-Buy went from 69.16 to 76.58.
  - Ablations show the explanations must be instance-specific. Generic dataset-level explanations lose 14.35 F1 and junk text loses 26.99.
- **Adaptation.** This is a France and shift-robustness trick:
  1. Generate short, instance-specific rationales for a few 10K train pairs with a **compliant ≤ 8B** model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct),
     using only our own train records. An example: "same street number and postcode; name differs only by legal suffix; region vs
     département is the same place".
  2. Train the band matcher with target = "label + rationale". At inference, score only the label token, so the rationale adds no
     serving cost.
  3. Check that the France slice (hold out India or a region as a pseudo-shift) improves.
- **Priority: P2** (P1 if the France slice is the dominant residual).

### 2.6 Pulsone, Shraga, Goren, *BEACON: Budget-Aware Entity Matching Across Domains* (SIGMOD 2026; arXiv 2603.11391)

- **What.** When target-domain labels are scarce, it chooses **out-of-domain training pairs by embedding distribution**:
  - Nearest-to-target-centroid;
  - **Train-Validation Distribution Fitting**, which picks OOD samples so that the training centroid moves toward the target
    (validation) centroid;
  - K-Center-Greedy coverage.

  It uses dynamic resampling on a Ditto/RoBERTa pipeline.
- **Primary.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11391 (the comment gives SIGMOD 2026); https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11391 (tables).
- **Evidence.** On WDC with 50 % corner cases and 50 % unseen products (weighted F1):

  | Method | 5k | 10k | Mean |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | BEACON | 0.758 | 0.763 | 0.739 |
  | SPEC | 0.710 | 0.731 | |
  | Jellyfish | 0.691 | | |
  | LLaMA-3.1 | 0.595 | | |

  They report +3.3 % average macro F1 over the next best.
- **Adaptation (transductive, and it uses no labels).** France has zero labels. We can:
  1. Embed candidate *pairs* (for example with our e5 embedding difference plus the GBDT feature vector) for France test candidates.
  2. Select or up-weight US/India training pairs whose pair-embeddings lie near the France pair distribution. France-like cases include
     region/département-style state mismatches, abbreviation-heavy addresses, "N°/bis/ter" numbers and diacritic-heavy names.
  3. Retrain or fine-tune the band model and the GBDT with those weights. This is importance weighting on covariates only, and it is
     cheap on CPU.
- **Priority: P1** for the France slice.

### 2.7 Ruan, Shi, Bauernhansl, *Fine-tuning LLMs with contrastive margin ranking loss for selective entity matching in product data integration* (Mistral4SelectEM; Advanced Engineering Informatics 67 (2025) 103538, doi:10.1016/j.aei.2025.103538)

- **What.** Selective (listwise) EM. A query entity is compared against its whole candidate set. The model is an LLM-based embedding
  model in a Siamese setup, trained with a **contrastive margin ranking loss (CMRL)** to separate true positives from similar hard
  negatives. Candidate sets are top-10 by blocking plus FAISS.
- **Primary.** Metadata was confirmed through the Semantic Scholar API (title, authors, venue, DOI). Repo:
  https://github.com/quickhdsdc/LLM4EntityMatching (**MIT**). The README lists Mistral-embedding / Linq-Embed-Mistral / all-mpnet-base-v2
  bases and the Amazon-Google, Walmart-Amazon and Abt-Buy datasets.
- **Evidence.** **UNVERIFIED.** ScienceDirect returned 403. The only numbers-free claim, "outperforms state-of-the-art pairwise EM
  approaches in both efficiency and performance", comes from a search snippet of the abstract. The README reports no numbers.
- **Adaptation.** A margin ranking loss over {true S1, top-k hard S1 negatives, NONE} for our listwise head. That is equivalent to the
  per-target softmax over k+1 options in §4 P1-B. **Check the license of whichever embedding base is used.** Linq-Embed-Mistral's license
  was not checked here; e5-mistral-7b-instruct is MIT per models.md.
- **Priority: P2.**

### 2.8 Chen et al., *SEED: Domain-Specific Data Curation With Large Language Models* (arXiv 2310.00749 v3, Apr 2024)

- **What.** An "LLM-as-compiler" that builds a hybrid pipeline from four modules and an optimizer (priority = (1−p_i)/C_i, with
  skyline pruning):
  - **CodeGen:** LLM-written code replaces LLM calls;
  - **CacheReuse:** vector-cache reuse of earlier answers;
  - **ModelGen:** a small model trained on LLM annotations;
  - **LLM:** a direct call.
- **Primary.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00749 (HTML v3). Venue UNVERIFIED (arXiv only as read).
- **Evidence (ER).**

  | Dataset | Config | F1 | LLM calls |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Amazon-Google | SEED Max | 79.2 | 22.4 % |
  | Amazon-Google | SEED Opt | (5 % accuracy gap) | 10.4 % |
  | Abt-Buy | SEED Max | 92.8 | 27.1 % |
  | Abt-Buy | SEED Opt | 88.0 | 2.9 % |

### 2B.1 Lin, *Cost-Aware Evaluation of Large Language Models for Ambiguous Product Entity Matching* (Zenodo working paper, compiled 2026-05-06, record dated 2026-05-08) [P2]

- **What.** An evaluation of LLMs as a **selective escalation layer**. They are used only on pairs where a tuned fuzzy matcher is uncertain, not on the whole pair distribution.
- **Primary.** https://zenodo.org/records/20089436. I downloaded the PDF (`Lin-LLM-Dedupe-Comparison-Study.pdf`, 11 pages) and read its text. License: CC-BY-4.0.
- **Technique.**
  - A weighted fuzzy score (title, brand, description, specs) is grid-searched on WDC Products validation (42,949 pairs). The best weights put everything on title.
  - Test pairs (4,399) with an *intermediate* fuzzy score form the ambiguous set: **2,518 pairs, 799 matches and 1,719 non-matches**, at the natural ratio.
  - The pairwise prompt is deliberately **conservative**: "non-match when evidence is weak or conflicting". Output is JSON {decision, confidence, explanation, evidence-field}.
  - Two hybrid rules are also tested: an AND verifier (fuzzy ∧ LLM) and an OR rule (recall).
- **Evidence (Tables 6, 7, 9, 12-14).**
  - Fuzzy rule: F1 0.445 (P 0.401 / R 0.501).
  - Non-LLM diagnostics on the same set: title-only 0.583, uniform weights 0.500, **logistic regression on the 4 similarities 0.596**.
  - LLM-only F1:

    | Model | P | R | F1 | Cost per 1K pairs |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | Claude Sonnet 4.6 | 0.982 | 0.941 | 0.961 | $3.95 |
    | Claude Haiku 4.5 | 0.980 | 0.937 | 0.958 | $1.30 |
    | Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite | 0.953 | 0.812 | 0.877 | $0.086 |

    The bootstrap CIs for Sonnet and Haiku overlap (0.951-0.971 vs 0.948-0.968).
  - All three models made **more FN than FP**, which the author attributes to the conservative prompt.
  - **Self-reported confidence is only a rough routing signal:**
    - ECE (all decisions): 0.062 (Sonnet), 0.088 (Haiku), **0.346 (Flash-Lite)**.
    - At a confidence ≥ 0.95 threshold, Sonnet's recall collapses to 0.078.
  - The author states the limits: the baseline has "only four field-level similarity scores", the data is product data only, and the models are hosted.
- **Adaptation.**
  1. This supports the **band-only architecture**. It does not size our gain, because our band baseline is a ~90-feature stacked GBDT at 0.987 macro-F0.5, not a 4-feature LR.
  2. **Never use verbalized confidence.** Score with token logits (`logit(Yes) − logit(No)`) and temperature-calibrate. Flash-Lite's ECE of 0.346 shows how far verbalized confidence can be off.
  3. A "conservative" instruction or prior moves the operating point toward precision, which suits F0.5. With a fine-tuned scorer the same effect comes from the fused threshold, so no prompt engineering is needed.
  4. Evaluate the **AND-verifier** decision as one option: accept only if the GBDT accepts *and* the band LM does not veto. That is a precision-only use of the LM.
- **Priority: P2** (pattern and calibration evidence; the models are non-compliant).

### 2B.3 Zeakis, Papadakis, Skoutas, Koubarakis, *AvengER: Ensembling and Fine-Tuning LLMs for SELECT Prompts in Entity Resolution* (ESWC 2025, LNCS, doi:10.1007/978-3-031-94575-5_17) [P2]

- **What.** Listwise **SELECT** prompts, "answer with the candidate number, or [0] if there is none", studied with open 7-8B models. The unsupervised variant uses ensembles and a 32B judge. The supervised variant fine-tunes a single 7-8B model.
- **Primary.** Author PDF: https://cgi.di.uoa.gr/~koubarak/publications/2025/AvengER__Ensembling_and_Fine_Tuning_LLMs_for_SELECT_Prompts_in_Entity_Resolution-1.pdf (19 pages, text extracted). Crossref confirms the DOI. Code: https://github.com/alexZeakis/AvengER.
- **Technique.**
  - Blocking: S-GTR-T5 + FAISS kNN.
  - **Post-blocking refinement:** ranking@N, **reciprocal pruning** (keep (e_i, e_j) only if each is in the other's top-k), or hybrid (a Flan-T5 MATCH filter then top-4).
  - Prompt variants: plain SELECT, EXPLAIN and CONFIDENCE.
  - Voting: majority, weighted, confidence, aggregated over 5 permutations, and a judge.
  - **Fine-tuning with candidate-order permutations** (Unsloth, one RTX 4090).
  - Data: 8 datasets (Abt-Buy, Amazon-Google, DBLP-ACM, IMDb/TMDb/TVDB, Walmart-Amazon, DBLP-Scholar). 300 labelled + 100 unmatched query entities for test, and the same numbers for train.
- **Evidence (F1, mean of 8 datasets).**
  - **Pre-trained, zero-shot SELECT.** With reciprocal pruning: Llama-3.1-8B 0.73, Mistral-v0.3 0.62, Orca-2 0.53.
    - Adding 2 random few-shot examples: Llama **0.73 → 0.63** (response size 107 → 1,011 chars).
    - Reciprocal pruning 0.73, against ranking@10 0.70 and hybrid@4 0.64.
  - **Ensembles (pre-trained).**
    - Aggregated-majority over 5 permutations: 0.77.
    - **Hybrid vote** (call a Qwen-2.5-32B judge only when the internal vote score is < θ=0.6, **12 %** of queries): 0.80.
    - Any-vote reaches recall 0.87 but precision 0.49 (F1 0.62). Majority vote gives precision 0.72 and F1 0.74.
  - **Fine-tuned (Table 5).**
    - Llama-3.1 0.72 → **0.88**. Mistral-v0.3 0.67 → **0.88**, instruct and base alike.
    - All four fine-tuned variants average P 0.89 / R 0.87.
    - **Fine-tuned Mistral-v0.3-instruct with the raw ranking@10 candidates (no refinement): 0.88.**
    - Fine-tuned majority ensemble 0.88 and any-vote 0.87, which shows "low levels of complementarity".
    - **Position-bias variance over 10 permutations:** 0.0061 (fine-tuned Mistral) and 0.0069 (fine-tuned Llama), against 0.0116 (pre-trained Llama).
  - **Against the ComEM baseline (Table 6):** GPT-3.5 hybrid@4 0.86; pre-trained Mistral-v0.1 ranking@10 0.74. AvengER's unsupervised best is 0.80, and **fine-tuned Mistral-v0.3 is 0.88**.
- **Adaptation.** This is direct, compliant evidence for pass-1 plan P1-B.
  1. Use **Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3** (Apache-2.0, 7.25B, English/French-capable but weak on Indic) or **Qwen3-4B** (better for Indic).
  2. Target + top-k S1 candidates (k up to 10 is fine after fine-tuning), with `[0]` = NONE.
  3. **Train with shuffled candidate orders.**
  4. Output only an index token.
  5. No few-shot examples.
  6. No LLM ensembles, because fine-tuned models do not complement each other.
  - Before fine-tuning, reciprocal pruning is the cheapest way to shorten the candidate list. We already have reverse (target→S1) retrieval, so a "mutual top-k" flag is a cheap **CPU feature for the GBDT**, if it is not already in the 87 features.
- **Priority: P1** (conditional on GPU) for the listwise head. **P0** for the mutual-top-k feature check (CPU).

### 2B.4 Ather, *LLM-assisted record linkage: A framework for official statistics* (Statistical Journal of the IAOS 42(1):211-217, 2026-02-26; Statistics Canada; doi:10.1177/18747655261422068) [P2]

- **What.** This is a national statistics office's framework. Open-source LLMs are benchmarked against string comparators, and the **LLM is then used only for ambiguous record pairs inside a probabilistic (Fellegi-Sunter-style) linkage**.
- **Primary.** I read the abstract from the Crossref record for the DOI (the SAGE page returned 403). The ISI WSC 2025 abstract page (https://www.isi-next.org/abstracts/submission/3897/view/) gives no numbers.
- **Technique (abstract).**
  - "A Bayesian update is applied to combine the LLM's output with the prior probability", with the aim of reducing manual clerical review.
  - "Selectively deploying open-source LLMs for the most uncertain pairs can significantly reduce manual effort".
  - It also discusses on-premises deployment, cost, human-in-the-loop review and calibration.
- **Evidence.** **No numbers are available** to me (full text paywalled). Model names and sizes are **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Adaptation. This is the fusion rule to adopt.**
  - Treat the GBDT stage-2 probability as the prior, and the band LM's calibrated log-likelihood ratio as evidence:
    `logit p_post = logit p_gbdt + w · (s_LM − b)`, where `s_LM = logit(Yes) − logit(No)`.
  - Fit w and b by logistic regression on out-of-fold **band** pairs.
  - Pairs outside the band keep p_gbdt unchanged.
  - Then re-tune the threshold on P-dense.
  - This keeps the calibrated, density-aware decision layer intact, and it degrades gracefully: w → 0 when the LM adds nothing.
- **Priority: P0** for the design and a CPU dry run (the same fusion code can be exercised now with an existing second model, for example an e5-cosine score). P1 once an LM score exists.

### 2B.6 Steiner & Bizer, *Automatic End-to-End Data Integration using Large Language Models* (arXiv 2603.10547; accepted at the Beyond SQL workshop at ICDE 2026) [P2]

- **What.** GPT-5.2 generates all the artifacts of an integration pipeline: schema mappings, value mappings, **EM training data via active learning**, and fusion validation data. Three case studies, one of them **companies** (Forbes / DBpedia / FullContact).
- **Primary.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10547 (PDF text extracted; Table IV and §V read).
- **Technique (EM).**
  - Blocking: text-embedding-3-small kNN (k=20).
  - Seed labels: the LLM labels pairs in decreasing-similarity order until each query has ≥ 1 positive and 2 negatives, plus 2 bottom-ranked candidates. That gives about 100 seed pairs per dataset pair.
  - Active learning: select 100 pairs per round where a committee (RF / XGB / GB / HGB / LR on string-similarity features) has the highest prediction variance.
  - **The final training set is augmented with 20 % randomly sampled pool pairs "to counteract the selection bias toward difficult pairs".**
  - Cluster cleanup by maximum bipartite filtering.
- **Evidence (Table IV, F1 on held-out test sets, identical matchers).**

  | Dataset pair | Human config | Human labels | LLM labels |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Companies: DBpedia-Forbes | .857 | .954 | .939 |
  | Companies: Forbes-FullContact | .870 | .898 | .897 |
  | Average over 6 pairs | .894 | .916 | .937 |

  Active learning labelled 600 to 2,658 pairs per dataset pair, for $4.95 over all three case studies. Labelling all candidates (up to 837K pairs) would have cost about $466.
- **Adaptation.**
  - We have gold labels, so the teacher part does not apply. It would also be a hosted model.
  - Transferable details:
    1. The **committee-variance selector** over our own LightGBM fold models, to build the band-LM training set.
    2. **Add about 20 % random pairs** to that set, so the band LM stays calibrated outside the hardest region.
    3. Here the GBDT trained on string similarities was the final matcher. That is further evidence that a strong feature GBDT is a legitimate end state.
- **Priority: P2.**

### 2B.7 Qiao et al., *LLM-based Listwise Reranking under the Effect of Positional Bias* (DebiasFirst; ECIR 2026, doi:10.1007/978-3-032-21289-4_9; arXiv 2604.03642) [P2]

- **Primary.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03642 (abstract). The ECIR venue comes from the Springer chapter listing in search results.
- **Technique.** Two sources of positional bias are identified: architectural bias, and the **imbalanced position of the relevant item** in training lists. The fix:
  - (a) **positional calibration by inverse propensity scoring** in the fine-tuning loss;
  - (b) **position-aware augmentation**, so every item appears equally often in every position.
- **Evidence.** The abstract reports reduced dependence of NDCG@10 on the position of relevant documents, with no numbers in the abstract.
- **Adaptation.**
  - Our blocking order puts the true S1 at rank 1 most of the time. A listwise head trained on GBDT-ordered candidates would learn "pick slot 1".
  - **Shuffle candidate order uniformly in training** (AvengER's permutation training), or IPS-weight by the empirical rank distribution of the gold S1.
  - At inference, optionally average over 2 permutations.
- **Priority: P2** (only matters once P1-B is built).

### 2B.9 Jang, Kim, Ko, *HELEA: Hard-Negative Benchmark and LLM-based Reranking for Robust Entity Alignment* (arXiv 2605.28308, May 2026) [P2]

## 4. Concrete adaptation plan for our pipeline (pass 2 revision)

**Gating.** Compute is blocked (Modal spend limit; GPU needs billing), and the laptop must not run models. So:
- **P0 = CPU-only, can run as soon as Modal CPU is back.** These items use existing OOF predictions and features.
- **P1 = build only if (a) GPU is available *and* (b) P0-1 shows band headroom of at least about 0.002 absolute macro-F0.5 on P-dense.** That is about 20 % of the remaining gap. The threshold is my judgment, not from a paper.
- Features v2 (house-number geometry) and normalization v2 come **before** every item below, because they target the dominant residual FP class directly (§1 table).

### P0: cheap, and decides whether an LLM stage is worth building

**P0-1 Band headroom oracle, split by residual error class.**
- **Band definitions** (report each): OOF stage-2 p ∈ [0.15, 0.85]; top-2 margin < 0.2; the decision-flip set under ±δ around the tuned threshold. **Add the "confident-wrong" set** (p > 0.9 with y=0, or p < 0.1 with y=1; ALER, 2B.8).
- **Oracle.** Replace band decisions with gold and recompute exact macro-F0.5 on P-main **and P-dense**.
- **Report:**
  - band size in train OOF, and the implied test band pair count out of 88.4M;
  - gain per slice: sibling-lookalike FP, empty-address FN, alias/DBA, web-handle, acronym, cross-script, truncation, NONE/distractor;
  - LOCO (US→India and India→US) as the France proxy.
- **Rationale.** Every source shows in-domain LLM gains of about 0 to 5 F1 at best (§3.5). If the oracle gain sits almost entirely in the sibling or empty-address classes, the LM is unlikely to capture it (§1 table).

**P0-2 Shortcut audit (Beyond Scale Semi-Rel; HELEA same-name negatives).**
- For negatives in the train candidate pool against the test candidate pool (unlabelled, using candidate features only), compute per country, including France test, the rate of:
  - (a) exact or near-exact normalized-name agreement with a different address;
  - (b) the same street with a different house number;
  - (c) the same address with a different name;
  - (d) generic-token-only agreement.
- If the rates differ by more than about 2×, rebalance or up-weight those negatives for any neural band model, and prefer smaller models.

**P0-3 F1id / F1none diagnostic (CaRL-EM).** Split validation macro-F0.5 losses into:
- wrong-S1 selections (FP+FN);
- missed matches;
- accepted distractors (NONE errors).

This decides whether a band model needs a better *selector* or a better *NONE detector*.

**P0-4 Log-odds (Bayesian) fusion harness (Ather 2026; Kamsteeg in fmeasure.md).**
- Implement `logit p_post = logit p_gbdt + w·(s − b)` on band pairs only, fitted by LR on OOF.
- Then re-tune the threshold and exclusivity on P-dense.
- Dry-run it now with any existing secondary score (e5/potion cosine, or a small CPU model) so that the plumbing and the P-dense re-tune exist before an LM arrives.
- **Never use verbalized LLM confidence** (Lin 2026: ECE up to 0.346).

**P0-5 Mutual-top-k (reciprocal) feature check (AvengER).**
- If the 87 features do not already include "S1 ∈ target's top-k **and** target ∈ S1's top-k" for several k (the reverse view exists), add it.
- In AvengER, reciprocal pruning beat one-directional top-10 by 0.03 mean F1 for an untuned 8B selector (0.73 vs 0.70).

**P0-6 Full-scale validation discipline (Shrestha 2026; contests.md #2).**
- Freeze the band-model evaluation to the full OOF band plus P-dense.
- No decisions on small samples. No voting or self-consistency.

### P1: main build (only if gated in)

**P1-A Band-only generative yes/no matcher (pairwise).**
- **Model:** Qwen3-1.7B *base* (Apache-2.0, 2.03B), with Qwen3-0.6B as fallback. Qwen3-4B only for the most uncertain ~10-20 % of the band. **Not Qwen3-8B** (8.19B, over the cap).
- **Input:** raw and normalized/romanized fields side by side (OpenSanctions), with **house number, street, postcode, city, state and country as separate fields** (HELEA lesson). Also include a few of our strongest feature values as text (for example `same_postcode=yes`).
- **Score:** a single forward pass, then `logit(" Yes") − logit(" No")`. Temperature-calibrate, then fuse via P0-4. No `generate`.
- **Training set** (all gold train pairs, out-of-fold):
  - the GBDT uncertain band;
  - **plus confident-wrong pairs** (ALER);
  - **plus about 20 % random pool pairs** (Steiner & Bizer ICDE-W 2026);
  - up-weight same-name / same-street / different-number negatives (P0-2).

  About 50 to 200K pairs, with positives capped at about 40 %. No few-shot prompting (Deep Dive Table 4; AvengER).
- **LoRA:** r=8, α=16 on all linear layers (Beyond Scale). 1 to 2 epochs.
- **Serving:** length-sorted batches in bf16, forward-only; dedupe identical normalized (target, S1) strings. **Benchmark 10K pairs on our stack first:** the 0.6B estimate is about 52 min per 1M pairs on an H100 (Beyond Scale), against 24.3 h measured by Steiner & Bizer.

**P1-B Per-target listwise "select one S1 or NONE" head (AvengER, CaRL-EM, ComEM).**
- **Model:** Qwen3-4B (Indic-capable) or Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (Apache-2.0; better on French than on Indic). Fine-tuned; AvengER shows zero-shot 7-8B selectors are far weaker (0.67-0.73 vs 0.88).
- **Input:** the target plus its top-k S1 candidates (k ≤ 10, after mutual-top-k ordering), each with index [1..k], and **[0] = NONE**.
- **Training:**
  - cross-entropy over k+1 index tokens;
  - **uniformly shuffled candidate order** (AvengER permutations);
  - or IPS weighting by the gold-rank distribution (DebiasFirst).
- **Inference:** logits masked to the k+1 index tokens, with 1 to 2 permutations averaged.
- **Outputs as features:** P(NONE) feeds the match-exists model, and P(S1_i) feeds the pairwise fusion.
- **Select-then-verify (CaRL-EM):** before acceptance, check the chosen S1 against the P1-A score or the GBDT through the fused threshold.
- **One target per prompt** (CE-RAG4EM batch-generation precision loss). No LLM ensembles (AvengER: fine-tuned models do not complement each other).

**P1-C France robustness.**
- BEACON-style distribution-aware reweighting of US/India training pairs toward the France test candidate-pair distribution. This works for the GBDT too and runs on CPU; it could be P0 if a CPU slot is free.
- If GPU is available, **prefer the generative LM for France.** Deep Dive: +14.6 mean F1 over Ditto cross-dataset, and 99.6/98.2 on restaurant data. Beyond Scale: retention 83.6 %.
- Validate on LOCO before trusting anything on France.

### P2: targeted add-ons

- **P2-1 Explanation-augmented training (Wadhwa 2024).** Label + rationale targets written by a compliant ≤ 8B model from our own records; only the label is scored at inference. Accept only if LOCO improves.
- **P2-2 Transductive pseudo-labels** on France and cross-script test candidates, agreement-filtered across ≥ 2 compliant scorers; SFT only. **Check the rules on transductive use first.**
- **P2-3 Post-LM hard vetoes** (ER-in-practice): country mismatch rejects; a clean conflicting postcode or house number rejects unless both GBDT and LM are very confident. Also evaluate Lin's **AND-verifier**: accept only if the GBDT accepts and the LM does not veto.
- **P2-4 Query-dedupe cache** for identical normalized pairs (SEED CacheReuse). Measure the duplicate rate first, because S2/S3 copies are noisy and not identical.
- **P2-5 Use F1none/F1id** (P0-3) for listwise model selection.
- **P2-6 Committee-variance selection** of band-LM training pairs over the LightGBM fold models (Steiner & Bizer ICDE-W 2026; Steiner & Bizer 2026 active learning).
- **P2-7 Rule-based sibling negatives** (HELEA; LLM-HN is the LLM version and is not allowed). Synthesize extra negatives from our own train S1 records: same name and street, house number ± small delta, same city. Up-weight them for the band model **and** the GBDT. Check first that the generator's planted siblings are not already enough (P0-2).
- **P2-8 DebiasFirst IPS** in the listwise loss, if order shuffling alone leaves a rank-1 bias.

### P3: only if time remains

- A CaRL-EM RL controller (our GBDT margin routing is simpler and adequate).
- DSPy/MIPRO prompt optimization (OpenSanctions: +1.89 F1). Few-shot prompting is harmful or neutral (Deep Dive, AvengER, Shrestha).
- Multi-step reasoning prompts (Bopardikar 2025) and LLM self-explanations as features (unfaithful per Teofili 2026).
- LLM ensembles and judges (AvengER hybrid voting 0.80 < a single fine-tuned model at 0.88).
- Per-slice model repositories (MoRER), and LLM-written normalization code or value maps (SEED CodeGen; Steiner & Bizer ICDE-W 2026). These are only legal with a compliant model and our own data.

## 5. Risks and open questions

1. **Compute.** Modal is blocked. Every P1 item needs GPU hours we do not have right now. Do the P0 items and features v2 first. They have value whether or not an LM is ever trained.
2. **Ceiling from the generator.** The generator shifts the house numbers of *true* copies and plants same-street siblings, so part of the residual is undecidable from the pair alone. An LM cannot exceed the information in the records. The P0-1 per-class oracle tells us how much is decidable.
3. **Param cap.** Qwen3-8B (8.19B) and Qwen3-Reranker-8B exceed 8.0B. Many 2026 papers report their best numbers with Llama-8B or Qwen3-8B. Our ceiling is Qwen3-4B, Mistral-7B-v0.3 or Qwen2.5-7B.
4. **Teacher legality.** Any hosted or > 8B teacher is out under a conservative reading. That covers DistillER, Steiner & Bizer (both papers), MERGED, LLM-HN and Lin.
5. **Throughput variance** across stacks is 28× (Beyond Scale estimate vs Steiner & Bizer measurement). Benchmark before sizing the band.
6. **Precision under scale and density.** Larger heads learn name shortcuts (Beyond Scale). Test density makes mid-range probabilities over-confident (PROJECT_STATE). Always fuse, re-calibrate and re-threshold on P-dense.
7. **Unverified content.** Ather 2026 (numbers), Karapiperis KBS 2026 (content), LLM-HN (all content), Mistral4SelectEM (numbers), AssistEM (paper), FUSER, Eedi write-up. None of these drive a P0 or P1 decision alone.
